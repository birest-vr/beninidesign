Interior design in Verona, with a site visit

When a project touches walls, services and bespoke joinery, being there changes everything. We survey, we verify, and where needed we are on site when the decisions that cannot be undone get made.

When in-person makes sense

  • Full renovation. Some decisions — chases, drains, slab levels — have to be made looking at an open wall.
  • Historic or irregular buildings. In central Verona, Valpolicella and the lowland courtyards, out-of-square walls are the rule: 3, 4, sometimes 6 centimetres.
  • Extensive bespoke joinery. Every wrong centimetre is a piece that doesn't fit and that nobody refunds.
  • Suspected damp. Rising damp, condensation, thermal bridges: diagnosed by touching the wall, not by looking at a photograph.
  • No time. A proper survey takes two or three focused hours. If you don't have them, we do.

What happens during the visit

0130 min

Conversation in the home

How you use the spaces, what doesn't work, what you want to keep. Things come up in the home that never come up on the phone.

0260–120 min

Measured survey

Laser distance meter, every wall dimension, diagonals, heights at several points, openings, reveals, visible services, wall thicknesses.

0320 min

Photographic and material survey

Complete documentation, sampling of existing materials, natural light checked by orientation and time of day.

0420 min

Constraint check

Damp, flatness, window condition, drain and stack positions, flues. It is a visual survey for the interior design.

053–5 days

Deliverables

You receive the dimensioned plan as a PDF with all real dimensions, a constraints report and a fixed-price design quote.

Local knowledge

The Verona homes we meet most often

Every building type has recurring constraints. Knowing them in advance avoids months of surprises.

Historic centre apartments

High ceilings, often with exposed beams or vaults. Heritage constraints on façades and sometimes on floor structures. Services usually need full replacement and there is very little room for storage. The recurring theme is lighting without drilling: tracks, pendants and floor lamps.

1960s–70s blocks

Corridor layouts with small rooms, a separate kitchen and a single bathroom. The work is almost always redistribution: removing the kitchen–living wall, adding a second bathroom, turning the corridor into storage. Watch for hollow-core concrete floors and legacy flue materials.

1980s–90s houses

Generous floor areas but dated layouts: basement taverna, a "best room" nobody uses, oversized bedrooms. Here the most effective intervention is functional before aesthetic — rethinking uses — combined with a serious lighting upgrade.

Valpolicella farmhouses and courtyards

Exposed stone, timber trusses, level changes, small openings. The challenge is bringing in light without betraying the building, and heating substantial masonry. Coherent materials: warm timbers, lime, iron, natural fabrics. Avoid polished porcelain, which loses against real stone.

Lake Garda homes

Often second homes or short lets with remote owners. Constraints: humidity and airborne salinity, intense seasonal use, minimal maintenance. Design for durability and photogenic quality, with materials that tolerate being shut up for months.

New energy-efficient builds

Efficient envelope, mechanical ventilation, underfloor heating, often plasterboard walls. Specific constraints: you can't drill anywhere, heavy furniture needs fixings planned during construction, and underfloor heating restricts some parquets and thick rugs. Anchor points must be decided in advance.

Frequently asked

About the Verona service

Is the site visit free?

No, it costs €100, for a simple reason: it isn't a sales call, it's two or three hours of technical work that produces a document — the dimensioned plan with a constraints report — which is yours to keep even if you don't continue with us. If the work is then commissioned, the €100 is deducted from the quote.

Do you work with my builder or bring your own?

We are happy to work with yours. We have no contractors to place at all costs, and where we have a commercial relationship with one we say so openly. If you don't have anyone yet, we suggest two or three Verona firms we have worked well with, and you choose after comparing quotes.

How long does a full project take?

For an 80–100 m² apartment: 5–8 weeks of design, then 8–20 weeks of ordering and delivery, which is the longest and least compressible part. Bespoke kitchens typically run 8–14 weeks in production, upholstery 6–12, windows longer still. The ordering programme exists precisely to stop the home standing idle waiting for one item.

Can I start online and add a site visit later?

Yes, and it happens often. We always begin with the free introductory call, then possibly the orientation call, and if the photographs raise technical doubts worth verifying, we add a site visit mid-project. The price is the list price, with no penalty for changing format.
